How the Confidence Penalty Works

What is the Macro Penalty?

High-impact economic events (FOMC, CPI, NFP) cause significant market volatility and unpredictable price movements. Trading around these events carries extra risk because technical and sentiment signals become less reliable.

How it works: When today is within ±1 day of a scheduled event, the recommendation engine automatically subtracts 10 points from every candidate's confidence score. This makes recommendations harder to qualify (they need higher base scores to pass the confidence threshold).

Why: This conservative approach helps you avoid taking positions right before major announcements that could move markets sharply in either direction, regardless of what technicals or sentiment suggest.

Event Types

FOMC Meetings

Federal Open Market Committee meetings where interest rate decisions are made. High volatility expected around statement release (2:00 PM ET on the second day of two-day meetings).

CPI Release

Consumer Price Index data from the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Released monthly, typically in the second week. Major market mover for inflation expectations.

NFP (Non-Farm Payrolls)

Employment Situation report from BLS. Released on the first Friday of each month at 8:30 AM ET. Key indicator for economic health and Fed policy.
